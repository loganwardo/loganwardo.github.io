Project Description

My senior design team is developing a biomimetic prosthetic hand that uses electrical signals from the user's muscles to control movement of the hand. The project combines EMG sensing, signal processing, embedded systems, mechanical design, and motor control.

The overall goal of the project is to develop a prosthetic hand that can recognize user input and recreate useful hand movements while also investigating methods of providing feedback to the user.

My Role

My primary role on the project is EMG and signal processing research. I have been researching surface EMG sensors, signal acquisition, sensor placement, and how EMG signals can eventually be converted into commands for the prosthetic hand.

I have also researched the number of EMG channels that may be needed, possible microcontrollers, and the feasibility of designing a custom circuit or PCB for acquiring and processing the EMG signals.

EMG Signal Acquisition

Surface electromyography, or sEMG, measures the electrical activity produced by muscles. Because these signals are relatively small, the system needs circuitry that can acquire and condition the signal before it can be used by a microcontroller.

Part of my work has involved determining what sensors and supporting electronics could be used to obtain reliable signals from the user's forearm while keeping the system affordable and practical for the project.

Current Development

Our team has divided the project into several areas, including EMG sensing and control, mechanical hand development, and user feedback. We have ordered EMG sensors for testing and are continuing to determine how the sensor signals will interface with the control electronics.

We are also investigating whether designing our own EMG acquisition hardware would give us more control over the design while reducing the cost of the final system.

Skills and Knowledge Gained

  • EMG signal acquisition and processing
  • Analog sensor interfacing
  • Embedded system design
  • PCB design considerations
  • Engineering research
  • Team communication and project planning

Big Picture Contribution

The EMG portion of the project provides the connection between the user and the prosthetic hand. The goal is to take electrical activity generated by the user's muscles, process that information, and convert it into commands that can control the movement of the hand.

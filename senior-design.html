Project Description

For my senior design project, my team is developing a biomimetic prosthetic hand that uses electrical signals generated by muscles to control hand movements. The goal is to develop a prosthetic system that can interpret muscle activity and translate it into useful movements of the hand.

This project combines several areas of engineering, including analog circuit design, signal processing, embedded systems, motor control, and mechanical design. Our team is also investigating ways to provide feedback to the user through the prosthetic hand.

My Role

My primary role is EMG signal acquisition and processing. Electromyography (EMG) measures electrical activity produced by muscles. I am responsible for researching how these signals can be collected, conditioned, and eventually interpreted by a microcontroller to control the prosthetic hand.

More recently, I have been working on the design of a custom EMG acquisition circuit using KiCad. This involves designing an analog signal conditioning system that includes an instrumentation amplifier, filtering stages, and an analog-to-digital converter.

I am also helping evaluate the components, power requirements, and interface between the EMG circuitry and the rest of the prosthetic system.

Skills and Knowledge Gained

This project has given me the opportunity to apply concepts from my electrical engineering coursework to a real-world design problem. One of the biggest challenges has been understanding how to work with the relatively small electrical signals produced by muscles.

I have gained knowledge in analog signal conditioning, instrumentation amplifiers, active filters, EMG sensor placement, and analog-to-digital conversion. I have also begun developing PCB schematic design skills using KiCad.

Beyond the technical work, this project has helped me develop my engineering research, communication, and teamwork skills. Because the project involves several different subsystems, coordinating with other team members is important to ensuring that our designs can work together.

Big Picture Contribution

My work on the EMG subsystem is important because it provides the connection between the user and the prosthetic hand. Without a reliable way to acquire and process muscle signals, the prosthetic hand would not be able to respond properly to the user's intended movements.

The goal of my contribution is to develop an affordable and reliable method of converting muscle activity into electrical signals that can be used by the hand's control system. This will allow the rest of the team to focus on translating those signals into motor commands and physical hand movements.

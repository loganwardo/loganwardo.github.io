Project Description

For this project, my partner and I designed and built a two-channel headphone amplifier. The amplifier used two active bandpass filters to provide separate left and right audio channels with independent volume control.

The design was required to have a lower cutoff frequency of approximately 100 Hz, an upper cutoff frequency of approximately 15 kHz, and a passband gain of 20 V/V.

My Role

I worked with my project partner throughout the design, simulation, construction, and testing of the amplifier. This included selecting component values, analyzing the filter circuits, building the circuit, and comparing the measured results with our design requirements.

Design

The amplifier used LMC660 operational amplifiers and was powered using positive and negative 12 V supplies. Each audio channel contained a bandpass filter along with a potentiometer that allowed the volume of each channel to be adjusted independently.

We first calculated the resistor and capacitor values needed to produce the desired cutoff frequencies and gain. The circuit was then simulated before being constructed and tested.

Results

Measurement Target Measured
Lower Cutoff Frequency 100 Hz 99.6 Hz
Upper Cutoff Frequency 15 kHz 15.6 kHz
Passband Gain 20 V/V 20.26 V/V

The completed amplifier closely matched the original design requirements and was successfully tested using an audio speaker.

PTAT Temperature Sensor

Project Description

For this project, my partner and I designed a proportional-to-absolute-temperature (PTAT) temperature sensor. The goal was to create an analog circuit with a linear output voltage between 0 V and 1 V over a temperature range of 0°C to 50°C.

The project included both the circuit design and physical layout of the temperature sensor.

My Role

I worked with my project partner on the design, simulation, transistor sizing, circuit layout, and verification of the temperature sensor. This required combining concepts from throughout the course into one complete integrated circuit design.

Design

The first stage of the circuit used two diodes, a current mirror, and a resistor to generate a voltage that changed with temperature. The diode area ratio was selected to create the required PTAT behavior.

A differential amplifier and resistor network were then used to amplify and shift the signal so that the final output ranged from approximately 0 V at 0°C to 1 V at 50°C.

The circuit was designed and simulated in Cadence Virtuoso before the physical layout was completed.

Results

Measurement Target Result
Temperature Range 0–50°C 0–50°C
Output Range 0–1 V Approximately 0–1 V
Ideal Slope 0.020 V/°C 0.0199947 V/°C

The simulated sensor produced a nearly linear output across the required temperature range. The measured slope was very close to the ideal value of 0.020 V/°C.

Layout and Verification

After completing the schematic, the circuit was converted into a physical layout using Cadence Virtuoso. The layout included the transistors, resistors, diodes, and operational amplifier used in the design.

Design Rule Checking (DRC) and Layout Versus Schematic (LVS) were used to check the physical implementation and identify layout errors.
